How to animate stick figures (a quick guide)

Stick figure animation has always been the easiest way to start animating, because the rig is already built for you — you are posing and moving a character rather than drawing one from scratch. This stickman animation maker gives you two ways in: describe a scene in plain English and let it build the whole timeline instantly, or open the Pose Designer and hand-craft every frame yourself, the same way traditional stick figure animators like Pivot Animator or Stick Nodes work, just without installing anything.

Start with the scene builder, then fine-tune by hand

Typing something like "a stickman walks into a cafe, sits down, and drinks coffee" builds a full timeline instantly — background, actions, and timing included, using built-in keyword understanding rather than a live AI call. From there you can edit any segment, swap an action, or open the Pose Designer on a single character to hand-pose a custom move the built-in action list does not cover.

Design your own scene, step by step

The instant scene builder is the fast path, but the "Design Your Own Scene" tool is how you build a move nothing on the built-in action list covers — a specific punch combo, a signature dance, a custom sword stance. Here is the exact flow:

  1. Click Start designing → in the "Design Your Own Scene" banner near the top of the page (or pick "Design your own..." from any segment's action dropdown, or its pencil edit button, to edit an existing move).
  2. If your scene has more than one character, pick which one you are posing.
  3. On the live preview, drag the purple dots on the hands, feet, and head to pose the character — or use the sliders on the right for precise control. The background and every other character stay visible while you work.
  4. Happy with the pose? Click Add keyframe. Build a sequence by adding a few keyframes in order — for example guard, then jab, then kick, then back to guard — and the tool smoothly animates between each one on a loop.
  5. If the move should travel across the stage (walking, a running punch, retreating), check "This move travels across the screen" and set a direction and speed. If it should also rise or fall (a jump, a duck, a climb), check that too.
  6. Click Save move. It now plays back as a real segment in your timeline, exactly like a built-in action.
  7. Want to reuse it later? Save it to your Move Library so it is one click away on any character, in any scene, going forward.
  8. Repeat for any other characters or segments, then hit Export Video to download the finished .webm.

Spacing, holds, and anticipation

Three ideas separate animation that reads as alive from animation that reads as robotic. Spacing is how far a joint travels between frames — even spacing looks mechanical, while a punch that accelerates toward impact and then briefly holds still reads as forceful. Anticipation is a small counter-movement before a big one, like crouching slightly before a jump, which gives a viewer a visual cue that something is about to happen. Every built-in action and pose in this tool already applies these principles, so a custom move you hand-pose in the Pose Designer will blend in naturally next to them.

Fight scenes, weapons, and effects

Give a character a sword, katana, pistol, or AK47 from the Accessory list, then set their action to Sword Slash or Shoot. When another character is standing nearby, the effect actually lands on them — bullets travel to the target and spark on impact, and a sword slash paints a splash of color at the moment of contact. Combined with the Fight, Kick, and Argue actions, this is enough to block out a full duel or action scene without touching a single keyframe by hand.
